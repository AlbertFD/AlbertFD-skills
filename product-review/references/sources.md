# Sources & trust guide

Read this before searching. It defines what counts as a credible source in each tier, how to detect fake or incentivized reviews, and what to reject outright. The goal is a verdict the user can spend money on.

## The three tiers (gather from all three)

A good verdict triangulates: when retailer crowds, independent testers, and real owners agree, confidence is high; when they disagree, that disagreement is itself the story.

### Tier 1 — Retailer reviews (the crowd)
Aggregate ratings and verified-buyer reviews from where the product is sold.

- Good: Amazon, Best Buy, Walmart, Target, B&H, Newegg, the manufacturer's own store, and the dominant retailers for the user's region (e.g. Argos/Currys UK, MediaMarkt EU, Flipkart IN).
- Always capture **rating + review count + distribution**, not just the headline average. A 4.3 from 12,000 reviews is robust; a 4.8 from 9 is noise. Read the 1–3★ reviews — defects and dealbreakers live there.
- Treat the manufacturer's own store reviews with extra caution (moderation bias).

### Tier 2 — Expert / pro reviews (the testers)
Independent outlets that do controlled, hands-on testing.

- Good: Wirecutter (NYT), RTINGS, Consumer Reports, CNET, TechRadar, Tom's Hardware/Guide, DPReview (cameras), AnandTech, The Verge, What Hi-Fi, Serious Eats / America's Test Kitchen (kitchen), OutdoorGearLab, plus the recognized specialist press for the category.
- These catch what the crowd can't: lab measurements, durability tests, head-to-head comparisons.
- Distinguish genuine testing from re-skinned spec sheets. "Best X of 2026" affiliate listicles with no hands-on testing are weak — use them only for shortlisting, not for the verdict.

### Tier 3 — Community / forums (the owners)
Unfiltered long-term sentiment from real owners.

- Good: relevant subreddits (search `site:reddit.com`), category forums (e.g. Head-Fi, MTBR, AVForums), and substantive YouTube reviews (especially long-term/"X months later" videos).
- This is where you find return reasons, failures after 6–12 months, and honest cross-shopping.
- Weight by specificity: "mine died after 8 months, here's the photo" beats "love it!!".

## Detecting fake / incentivized reviews

A retailer average is only as honest as the reviews under it. Actively screen before trusting it:

- **Incentivized disclosure**: phrases like "received free in exchange for an honest review", "discounted for my opinion", or Vine-style tags. Incentivized reviews skew positive — discount them.
- **Rating-count mismatch**: thousands of reviews on a product that launched weeks ago, or a brand-new no-name brand with a suspiciously high count — classic review-padding.
- **Burst patterns**: a cluster of 5★ reviews on the same few days, often right after launch or a price event.
- **Repetitive / generic phrasing**: many reviews echoing the same wording or reading like AI/templated copy ("This product exceeded my expectations and the quality is amazing").
- **Reviewer profile**: accounts that only ever post 5★ reviews, or review unrelated products in bursts.
- **Distribution shape**: a "J-curve" (almost all 5★ and 1★, little in between) can indicate manipulation plus retaliatory dumps.
- **Cross-check tools**: Fakespot- / ReviewMeta-style analyses and journalism about a brand's review practices, if available this session.

If the retailer rating looks gamed, **say so explicitly** in the report's red-flags section and lean the verdict on expert + community evidence instead.

## What to reject

- The manufacturer's marketing copy presented as a review.
- Pure affiliate/SEO content farms with no hands-on testing and no author accountability.
- Single-source claims dressed up as consensus.
- Sponsored YouTube/blog content not disclosed as such (and discount disclosed sponsorships).
- Drop-shipping listings and obvious rebadges where the "brand" is meaningless.

## Sourcing alternatives

Alternatives must be **real, currently-available** products you can source this session. Useful archetypes to cover: a cheaper "good enough" pick, a direct rival at the same price, and a premium step-up. Expert "best X" guides and forum "I chose Y over X because…" threads are the best places to find genuine cross-shop options.
