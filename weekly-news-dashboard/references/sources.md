# Reputable sources & verification

Read this before gathering stories. The whole value of the briefing rests on provenance, so the bar for what counts as a source is deliberately high.

## What counts as reputable

Use sources with editorial standards, corrections policies, and a track record of accuracy. In rough priority order:

**Wire services (best for fast, factual confirmation)**
Reuters, Associated Press (AP), Agence France-Presse (AFP).

**Newspapers & magazines of record**
The New York Times, The Wall Street Journal, Financial Times, The Economist, The Guardian, BBC News, Bloomberg, The Washington Post. For analysis, these plus outlets like The Atlantic or Nature/Science news desks.

**Primary institutional sources (cite these directly when the story originates there)**
- Science: the journal itself (Nature, Science, The Lancet, NEJM, PNAS, PRL/APS), the lab or agency (CERN, NASA, ESA, ESO, NOAA, NIH), and IPCC/WHO for climate and health. arXiv/preprint servers are acceptable for physics/CS *developments* but must be labelled as preprints (not peer-reviewed) and ideally paired with reporting.
- Economics: central banks (Fed, ECB, BoE, SNB), IMF, OECD, World Bank, BIS, and official statistics offices (BLS, Eurostat, national stats agencies) for the underlying data behind any market story.
- Politics: official government/parliament/court publications and the wire services for breaking developments.

**Regional outlets of record (for the Local section)**
Pick the recognized quality outlets for the user's region, not whatever ranks first. Examples by region:
- Geneva / French-speaking Switzerland: RTS, Le Temps, Tribune de Genève, swissinfo (SWI), Le News (English).
- Switzerland (national): NZZ, SRF, swissinfo, Swissinfo English.
- UK regions: BBC regional, the relevant broadsheet.
- US metros: the city's major daily of record plus a public-radio affiliate.
For any region, prefer public broadcasters and established dailies; verify a local outlet's reputation before relying on it.

## What to reject

- Tabloids and sensationalist outlets (Daily Mail, NY Post, Bild, etc. for factual claims).
- Partisan advocacy sites and political blogs presented as news.
- Content farms, SEO aggregators, and AI-generated news sites.
- Press releases or vendor blogs treated as independent reporting (a company announcing its own result is a primary source for *that it announced*, not independent confirmation of the claim).
- Anything sourced only to social media posts, unless an institution's own verified account is the primary record (e.g. an agency announcement) — and even then, corroborate.

## Verification standard (reputable + verify)

1. **Two independent reputable sources per story.** Different organizations doing their own reporting. Reuters plus three papers that all ran the same Reuters wire = one source, not four. The point of the second source is independent confirmation.
2. **Confirm the specifics**, not just the topic: the headline claim, the key figure, the named people, and the date — all checked against what you actually saw this session.
3. **Real links only.** Every URL must be one you retrieved this session and that loads the described story. Never reconstruct or guess URLs/DOIs.
4. **Single-source stories** that are clearly important may be included but must be flagged `single-source` / developing in the data, never presented as confirmed.
5. **When you can't verify**, say so. A shorter briefing of solid stories beats a padded one with shaky entries.

## Significance over drama

Rank candidates by real-world impact: how many people are affected, whether it's a genuine development or incremental noise, and whether it changes a policy, a market, a scientific understanding, or daily life in the user's area. A quiet but consequential central-bank decision outranks a loud but trivial controversy.
