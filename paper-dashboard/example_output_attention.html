01What this paper achieved

The authors introduce the Transformer, a sequence-transduction architecture built entirely on attention mechanisms — no recurrence, no convolutions. It set new state-of-the-art translation quality while training far faster and more parallelizably than the recurrent and convolutional models that preceded it. It became the foundation of essentially all modern large language models.

From recurrence to pure attention

Dominant sequence models (RNNs, LSTMs, GRUs) process tokens one position at a time, which forbids parallelization within a sequence and lengthens training. The Transformer replaces recurrence with self-attention, letting every position attend to every other in a single step.

The result is constant O(1) path length between any two tokens — long-range dependencies are one hop away rather than O(n) recurrent steps apart.

Why it mattered

Beyond the headline BLEU gains, the Transformer is more parallelizable and reaches top quality at a fraction of the training cost of prior models. Its uniform attention-based design also generalizes: the authors show it works for English constituency parsing too.

This combination — quality, speed, generality — is why the architecture, not just the result, became the default substrate for NLP and later vision and multimodal models.

The four headline contributions

1 · A recurrence-free architecture

The Transformer relies solely on stacked self-attention and position-wise feed-forward layers in an encoder–decoder design (N = 6 layers each).

2 · Multi-head scaled dot-product attention

Attention is computed in parallel across h = 8 heads on scaled dot products, letting the model attend to information from different representation subspaces at once.

3 · New translation state of the art

28.4 BLEU on WMT'14 English→German (>2 BLEU over prior best, including ensembles) and 41.8 BLEU single-model on English→French.

4 · Far cheaper training

The big model reached these scores in 3.5 days on 8 P100 GPUs — a small fraction of the training cost of competing models; the base model trains in ~12 hours.

02Architecture, equation by equation

The Transformer is an encoder–decoder stack. Both are built from two repeating sublayers — multi-head self-attention and a position-wise feed-forward network — each wrapped in a residual connection and layer normalization: LayerNorm(x + Sublayer(x)). We unpack the core equations.

Equation 1 — Scaled dot-product attention

Attention maps a query and a set of key–value pairs to a weighted sum of the values, where the weight on each value is the compatibility of its key with the query:

\[ \mathrm{Attention}(Q,K,V) = \mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^{\top}}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V \]
QMatrix of queries — what each position is "looking for".
KKeys — what each position "offers" to be matched against queries.
VValues — the content actually aggregated once attention weights are set.
QKᵀDot-product similarity of every query with every key — the raw attention scores.
1/√dkScaling factor. For large d_k the dot products grow large and push softmax into low-gradient regions; dividing by √d_k counteracts this.
softmaxNormalizes scores into weights that sum to 1 across the attended positions.

Why scale by √dk?

The authors note that for small d_k additive and dot-product attention perform similarly, but for large d_k the unscaled dot products grow in magnitude, saturating the softmax and shrinking gradients. Scaling by 1/√d_k keeps variance controlled, recovering dot-product attention's speed and memory efficiency (it's just optimized matrix multiplication) without the gradient pathology.

Equation 2 — Multi-head attention

Rather than one attention function over full-dimensional keys/values/queries, the model linearly projects them h times into lower-dimensional subspaces, attends in parallel, and concatenates:

\[ \mathrm{MultiHead}(Q,K,V) = \mathrm{Concat}(\mathrm{head}_1,\dots,\mathrm{head}_h)\,W^{O}, \quad \mathrm{head}_i = \mathrm{Attention}(QW_i^{Q},KW_i^{K},VW_i^{V}) \]
hNumber of parallel attention heads (h = 8 in the base model).
WiQ,K,VPer-head projection matrices into dimension d_k = d_v = d_model/h = 64.
WOOutput projection mixing the concatenated heads back to d_model.
headiOne attention computation in its own representation subspace.

Why multiple heads

A single attention head averages over everything it attends to, blurring distinct relationships. Multiple heads let the model jointly attend to information from different representation subspaces at different positions — e.g. one head tracking syntactic dependencies, another co-reference. Because each head is lower-dimensional (64), the total cost is similar to single-head full-dimensional attention.

Equation 3 — Position-wise feed-forward network

Each layer also applies a small fully connected network to every position independently and identically:

\[ \mathrm{FFN}(x) = \max(0,\,xW_1 + b_1)\,W_2 + b_2 \]

Two linear transformations with a ReLU between them. Input/output dimension is d_model = 512; the inner layer is d_ff = 2048.

Equation 4 — Positional encoding

With no recurrence or convolution, the model has no inherent notion of order, so sinusoidal position signals are added to the input embeddings:

\[ PE_{(pos,2i)} = \sin\!\left(\frac{pos}{10000^{2i/d_{model}}}\right), \quad PE_{(pos,2i+1)} = \cos\!\left(\frac{pos}{10000^{2i/d_{model}}}\right) \]

Why sinusoids

Each dimension corresponds to a sinusoid of a different wavelength (geometric progression from 2π to ~10000·2π). The authors hypothesize this lets the model easily learn to attend by relative position, since PE(pos+k) is a linear function of PE(pos), and may extrapolate to sequence lengths longer than those seen in training. Learned positional embeddings performed nearly identically.

The three uses of attention

1

Encoder self-attention

Each position attends to all positions in the previous encoder layer.

2

Decoder self-attention

Masked so each position attends only to earlier positions, preserving auto-regression.

3

Encoder–decoder attention

Decoder queries attend over the full encoder output — the classic seq2seq attention.

03Training setup

The recipe is notable for how standard and lightweight it is — the architecture, not exotic training tricks, does the work.

A

Data

WMT 2014 English–German (~4.5M sentence pairs, 37k shared byte-pair-encoding tokens) and the larger English–French (~36M pairs, 32k word-piece vocabulary). Batches held ~25k source and 25k target tokens.

B

Hardware & schedule

One machine with 8 NVIDIA P100 GPUs. The base model trained for 100,000 steps (~12 hours, ~0.4 s/step); the big model for 300,000 steps (~3.5 days).

C

Optimizer

Adam (β₁ = 0.9, β₂ = 0.98, ε = 10⁻⁹) with a learning rate warmed up linearly over 4,000 steps then decayed proportionally to the inverse square root of the step number.

D

Regularization

Residual dropout P_drop = 0.1 (base) applied to each sublayer output and to embedding+PE sums, plus label smoothing ε_ls = 0.1 — which hurts perplexity but improves BLEU and accuracy.

Beyond translation

Applied to English constituency parsing (WSJ Penn Treebank), the Transformer reached 91.3 F1 in the WSJ-only setting and 92.7 F1 in the semi-supervised setting — competitive with or beating prior task-specific models, showing the architecture generalizes beyond machine translation.
